# Negotiating with an AI Supplier: Facilitator Guide

## Overview

Students act as a procurement manager at Brightline Robotics buying 5,000 TS-40 temperature sensors. Their counterpart is Dana Reyes, a sales manager at Northgate Components, played by an AI. Dana has a hidden walk-away price, trade-offs she values across several issues, and quota pressure she does not reveal unless asked well. Both companies are fictional.

The exercise gives every student a consistent, patient counterpart. It also prompts discussion of what changes, and what doesn't, when the other side of the table is an AI.

## Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

1. Prepare for and run a multi-issue negotiation: price, delivery, payment terms, warranty and contract length.
2. Find value-creating trades by asking about the other side's interests.
3. Explain how anchoring and the pattern of concessions shaped their result.
4. Assess the strengths and limits of using an AI as a negotiation counterpart or practice partner.

## Timing

| Segment | Minutes |
|---|---|
| Brief and preparation (students read the brief in the simulation) | 10 |
| Negotiation | 20–30 |
| Debrief | 30–45 |

For asynchronous use, assign the negotiation as homework and have students upload their downloaded transcript, with a 300-word reflection.

## Student information

The brief is built into the simulation page. Students know:
- List price is $42.00 per unit. Their budget target is $36.00 per unit. Their boss has authorized up to $38.00.
- They need delivery in 8 weeks or less for a product launch.
- Their finance team prefers net 60 payment terms.
- Forecast demand is roughly 12,000 units per year for the next two years, though not yet committed.

## Confidential: Dana's position

| Issue | Dana's preference | Flexibility |
|---|---|---|
| Price | Target $38.50 or better; walk-away $34.75 | Drops to $35.50 for a 2-year, 10,000+/yr agreement |
| Delivery | 10 weeks standard | 7 weeks for $1.50/unit rush fee; fee waived at $37.00 or above |
| Payment | Net 30 | Net 60 costs Dana about $0.60/unit |
| Warranty | 12 months | 24 months costs Dana about $0.40/unit |
| Hidden | Quarter ends in 3 weeks; $180,000 short of quota | Revealed only for good questions about timing |

**Integrative solution:** A 2-year agreement at about $35.50–36.00 with 7-week delivery, the rush fee waived or shared, and net 45–60 terms beats the student's budget target and helps Dana hit quota. Students who negotiate only on price usually end up at $37–38 with 10-week delivery, which misses their launch date.

## Debrief questions

1. What was your first offer, and what was Dana's? How did those anchors shape the rest of the negotiation?
2. Which issues did you trade? Which did you never raise?
3. Did you learn anything about Dana's interests (timing, quota, volume)? What question surfaced it?
4. Compare outcomes across the class (collect final price, delivery and term length on the board). Why do they vary so much against the same counterpart?
5. How did negotiating with an AI feel different from negotiating with a person? Did you behave differently: more aggressive, more patient, less concerned with the relationship?
6. Where would you trust an AI to negotiate on your behalf, and where not?

## Notes for instructors

- The AI's replies vary from run to run. Treat outcomes as data for discussion rather than as a right answer.
- Students may try to "jailbreak" Dana into revealing her numbers. That is worth discussing too: what does it say about negotiating with automated agents in practice?
- Student messages are sent to an AI provider (Anthropic) to generate Dana's replies. Tell students not to enter personal information. The library does not store conversations; students download their own transcript.
